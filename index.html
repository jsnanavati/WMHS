Call for Papers

We invite submissions on patient world models, intervention-aware modelling, clinical trial simulation, and the evaluation and deployment of generative models in high-stakes health settings; submissions may address any of the workshop's topic areas and may take the form of methodological research, empirical studies, benchmarks and datasets, working systems, or position and perspective papers.

Topics of Interest

We invite original submissions on AI for high-stakes healthcare.

  • Patient world models using longitudinal, multimodal clinical data
  • Clinical trial simulation, virtual trial arms, synthetic controls, and external control cohorts
  • Counterfactual outcome prediction, treatment effect modelling, and target trial emulation
  • Foundation models for EHR, clinical trials, real-world evidence, and patient timelines
  • Representation of interventions, treatment regimes, endpoints, eligibility criteria, pathways, and mechanisms of action
  • Causal representation learning, causal inference, and off-policy evaluation for intervention-aware simulation
  • Uncertainty quantification, calibration, abstention, ambiguity, and selective prediction
  • Temporal reasoning, logical consistency, clinical plausibility, and protocol-aware reasoning
  • Agentic systems for trial design, protocol interpretation, evidence synthesis, and clinical research workflows
  • Benchmarking, robustness, and validation against real-world evidence, historical trials, clinical knowledge, and downstream clinical utility

Submission Tracks

Research, demonstration, and position papers may address any of the three topic areas. We encourage multidisciplinary submissions involving stakeholders from healthcare, public policy, and adjacent fields.

Research Papers (up to 9 pages for full papers or 4 pages for extended abstracts)

Research papers should present methodological, empirical, or analytical contributions relevant to AI for high-stakes healthcare.

Full papers should describe complete, self-contained work with clearly stated claims and sufficient evidence to support them. Extended abstracts may present focused contributions, preliminary but substantiated findings, work in progress, negative results, or emerging research directions.

Suitable topics include:

  • Patient world models and longitudinal trajectory modelling;
  • Clinical trial simulation and virtual populations;
  • Intervention-aware, causal, and counterfactual modelling;
  • Treatment-effect estimation and synthetic controls;
  • Foundation models and generative models for health;
  • Benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation frameworks;
  • Calibration, uncertainty, robustness, and generalisation;
  • Safety, reliability, and failure-mode analysis.

Depending on the contribution, supporting evidence may include comparisons with relevant baselines, held-out or external validation, ablation studies, simulation experiments, clinical case studies, or systematic analyses of model behaviour.

Reviewers will assess:

  1. Technical soundness and methodological rigor;
  2. Quality and appropriateness of the evaluation;
  3. Novelty of the methods, findings, datasets, or benchmarks;
  4. Relevance to AI for high-stakes healthcare;
  5. Potential scientific, clinical, or translational impact;
  6. Clarity of presentation.

Full papers are not expected to match the breadth of a NeurIPS main-conference paper, but their central claims must be adequately supported. Extended abstracts will be assessed relative to their shorter format and will not be expected to provide the same breadth of evaluation.

Demonstration Papers (up to 4 pages)

Demonstration papers should describe a functioning system, prototype, application, or research tool relevant to AI for high-stakes healthcare.

The system may be deployed, under evaluation, in pilot use, or at a working prototype stage. Submissions should describe the system architecture, intended users, use context, and the problem it addresses.

A comprehensive quantitative evaluation is not required, but authors should provide evidence that the system operates as described. This may include:

  • Screenshots or example interactions;
  • Example outputs or case studies;
  • A link to an anonymized demonstration video;
  • Preliminary user feedback;
  • Usage data or deployment experience;
  • Technical evaluation of key system components.

Purely conceptual systems, interface mockups, or proposals without a functioning implementation are not suitable for this track.

Demonstrations may include clinical decision-support tools, trial-simulation platforms, virtual-patient systems, model-evaluation tools, interactive benchmarks, visualization systems, or deployment and monitoring infrastructure.

Reviewers will assess:

  1. Whether the system is functional and sufficiently developed;
  2. Relevance and usefulness to healthcare stakeholders;
  3. Technical interest and originality of the system design;
  4. Evidence that the system works as described;
  5. Practical, clinical, or scientific significance;
  6. Clarity of the proposed demonstration.

Accepted demonstration papers may be invited to provide a live or recorded demonstration in addition to the poster presentation.

Position Papers (up to 4 pages)

Position papers should advance a clear and well-supported argument concerning AI for high-stakes healthcare.

New experiments are not required, but the central position should be grounded in relevant evidence, such as prior research, regulatory precedent, clinical experience, documented deployments, policy analysis, or established methodological principles.

Suitable topics include:

  • Standards for evaluating patient world models;
  • Validation requirements for clinical trial simulation;
  • Appropriate uses and limitations of virtual populations;
  • Governance and regulation of generative and predictive health models;
  • Clinical adoption and responsible deployment;
  • Safety, accountability, and human oversight;
  • Data access, privacy, and representation;
  • Benchmarking and reproducibility;
  • Scientific priorities and open research questions.

Strong position papers should articulate a specific thesis, explain its significance, and engage seriously with plausible counterarguments, limitations, or alternative perspectives.

Purely speculative opinion pieces without substantive evidence or analysis are unlikely to be accepted.

Reviewers will assess:

  1. Clarity and significance of the central position;
  2. Strength and grounding of the argument;
  3. Engagement with counterarguments and limitations;
  4. Relevance to AI for high-stakes healthcare;
  5. Potential to stimulate productive discussion or influence research and practice;
  6. Clarity of presentation.

Submission Formats

Research papers may be submitted either as:

  • Full papers: up to 9 pages; or
  • Extended abstracts: up to 4 pages.

Demonstration and position papers should be submitted as extended abstracts of up to 4 pages.

All page limits exclude acknowledgments, references, and appendices. Submissions may be shorter than the maximum page limit and will not be disadvantaged on that basis.

All accepted submissions will be presented as posters. A subset will be selected for contributed oral, spotlight, or demonstration presentations.

Format

Submission format and requirements

All submissions must be formatted using the NeurIPS 2026 LaTeX style file.

For anonymous submissions, authors should use:

\usepackage{neurips_2026}

without additional options. The NeurIPS Paper Checklist is not required.

Submissions must comply with the page limit for their selected track:

  • Research Papers: up to 9 pages, or up to 4 pages for extended abstracts.
  • Demonstration Papers: up to 5 pages.
  • Position Papers: up to 5 pages.

Extended abstracts are intended for focused contributions, preliminary but substantiated findings, emerging research directions, negative results, working demonstrations, and well-grounded position statements that do not yet warrant a full paper. Extended abstracts are reviewed against the same scientific standards but with an expectation of narrower scope.

All page limits exclude acknowledgments, references, and appendices. Figures and tables included in the main paper count toward the page limit.

The main submission should be self-contained and contain the evidence necessary to support its central claims. Reviewers are not required to read the appendix or supplementary materials.

Submissions that materially violate the formatting, anonymity, or page-limit requirements may be rejected without consideration of their merits.

We encourage multidisciplinary submissions involving researchers and practitioners from machine learning, statistics, causal inference, healthcare, clinical development, clinical trial methodology, biomedicine, public policy, regulation, pharmaceutical and biotechnology research, real-world evidence, and adjacent fields.

Given the high-stakes nature of healthcare applications, authors are encouraged to discuss relevant limitations, uncertainty, potential failure modes, ethical considerations, data governance, and risks associated with clinical or operational use.

Supplementary materials

Authors may include an optional appendix within the submission PDF. The appendix may contain additional experimental results, proofs, derivations, implementation details, figures, tables, or supporting analyses.

Additional anonymized supplementary materials, including code, data, videos, interactive demonstrations, or model outputs, may be submitted separately through OpenReview where supported.

Supplementary materials must not reveal the authors' identities during the double-blind review process. The main paper must remain understandable and sufficiently supported without requiring reviewers to inspect supplementary material.

Review

Review process

Submission platform

All submissions will be managed through OpenReview. Use the track links above to open the submission portal for your paper.

All submitting authors must have an OpenReview account with a complete profile. Account creation and profile verification can take several days, so authors are strongly encouraged to register early.

Double-blind review

The workshop will use a double-blind review process.

Submissions must be fully anonymized and must not contain author names, affiliations, identifying acknowledgments, or links that reveal the authors' identities.

Authors should refer to their own previous work in the third person and should ensure that repositories, project pages, supplementary files, document metadata, and video links do not disclose identifying information.

Anonymity violations may result in desk rejection.

Reviews and decisions

Each submission will receive three reviews from members of the program committee. Reviewers will be matched based on relevant technical, methodological, and domain expertise.

There will be no formal author-response or rebuttal stage. Given the short interval between the submission and notification deadlines, decisions will be based on the submitted paper, reviewer assessments, and discussion among the program committee and non-conflicted workshop organizers.

Authors should therefore ensure that their submissions are self-contained and clearly explain:

  • the central research question, system, or position;
  • the evidence supporting the principal claims;
  • relevant assumptions and limitations;
  • the relationship to patient world models, clinical trial simulation, or other high-stakes healthcare settings;
  • and any implications for validation, safety, responsible deployment, or clinical use.

Conflicts of interest

Reviewers and organizers will not participate in the evaluation of submissions for which they have a conflict of interest.

Conflicts may include current or recent institutional affiliations, advisor–student relationships, close collaboration, personal relationships, or other circumstances that could reasonably affect impartiality.

Submissions involving an organizer or an organizer's institution will be handled exclusively by non-conflicted organizers and reviewers.

Confidentiality

Submissions and supplementary materials will be visible only to assigned reviewers, relevant program committee members, and workshop organizers during the review process.

Reviewers must treat all submitted materials as confidential and may not use or distribute unpublished ideas, results, code, or data contained in a submission.

Rejected submissions will not be made public by the workshop.

Publication Policy

Non-archival status and dual submission

World Models for High-Stakes Health is a non-archival workshop.

Accepted papers will not be included in the official NeurIPS proceedings and will not constitute formal archival publications. Acceptance represents recognition and presentation within the workshop community rather than publication of record.

Authors retain copyright to their work.

Subsequent submission to other venues

Authors may submit the same work, or an extended version of it, to an archival conference or journal after the workshop.

Because the workshop is non-archival, presenting work at World Models for High-Stakes Health does not by itself preclude subsequent submission to venues such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, CHIL, ML4H, or relevant journals.

Authors remain responsible for complying with the submission, anonymity, prior-publication, and dual-submission policies of any subsequent venue.

Concurrent submissions

Work that is simultaneously under review at another venue may be submitted to the workshop, provided that doing so does not violate the other venue's dual-submission, confidentiality, anonymity, or public-posting policies.

Some venues may prohibit concurrent workshop submissions or public posting of papers under review. It is the authors' responsibility to check and comply with the applicable rules.

Previously published work

Papers that have already appeared in an archival conference proceeding or journal at the time of submission are not eligible.

Non-archival preprints, including papers posted on arXiv or similar repositories, do not count as prior publication and are permitted.

Prior non-archival workshop versions may also be submitted, provided that this does not violate the policy of the earlier workshop and that the submission is relevant to the aims of this workshop.

Work accepted to or presented as part of the NeurIPS 2026 main conference programme may not also be presented at this workshop.

Public availability of accepted papers

Following the camera-ready stage, accepted papers will be made publicly available through OpenReview or the workshop website by default.

Authors may choose to opt out of public PDF posting. Instructions and a reminder will be provided before the camera-ready deadline.

Making an accepted paper publicly available through OpenReview or the workshop website is a courtesy listing and does not change the non-archival status of the workshop.

Authorship

Authorship policy

The author list entered at the submission deadline must be complete and accurate.

No authors may be added after the submission deadline. This applies during review, camera-ready preparation, and any subsequent workshop version of the paper.

Author reordering is permitted for accepted papers, provided that all listed authors consent to the change.

Removing an author after submission requires written consent from all listed authors, including the author being removed. Requests must be sent to the program chairs with a clear explanation.

These rules are necessary to preserve the integrity of conflict-of-interest checks and the double-blind review process.

Presentation

Presentation and awards

All accepted papers will be invited for poster presentation at the workshop.

A subset of accepted papers will be selected for contributed oral or spotlight presentations. Selections will be based on the quality of the contribution, relevance to the workshop, and potential value to the workshop discussion.

The workshop will present three awards:

  • Outstanding Research Paper Award.
  • Outstanding Demonstration Paper Award.
  • Outstanding Position Paper Award.

Awards will be made only where the program committee determines that submissions in the relevant track meet an appropriate standard. The organizers may choose not to make an award in a track if there are insufficient eligible submissions.

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for and present the work at the workshop. Any exceptions will be considered only in exceptional circumstances and subject to applicable NeurIPS workshop policies.
